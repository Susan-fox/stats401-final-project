US Flight Delays: From Symptoms to Source

Travelers blame the airport, the airline, or the weather. The Bureau of Transportation Statistics records a different leading cause — and it points inward, at the network's own schedule.

Dataset requirement 1

Raw data

Twelve monthly files from the BTS Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance database, covering January–December 2024. Each file is one row per scheduled domestic flight operated by a carrier accounting for at least 0.5% of domestic passenger revenue.

  • Source: BTS TranStats, database EFD (field definitions)
  • Acquisition: scripted download of the pre-packaged monthly archives from the /PREZIP/ endpoint, rather than the interactive form
  • Volume: ≈7 million rows × 110 columns, about 2.5 GB uncompressed
  • Secondary: OpenFlights airport database, trimmed to US airports with IATA codes, for the latitude and longitude that BTS does not supply

The raw files are not committed. data/raw/ is in .gitignore; scripts/build_data.py regenerates everything downstream of it.

Processing completed

We read only the 27 columns our questions need, using DuckDB to query the CSVs directly rather than loading them into memory. Four issues in this dataset needed decisions rather than defaults:

  1. The five cause columns are conditionally populated. They carry values only when arrival delay reaches 15 minutes. Blank is not zero, so every cause share is computed over delayed flights, never over all flights.
  2. Cancelled and diverted flights have null arrival delay. Dropping them silently would reward airports that cancel aggressively, so they are excluded from delay statistics and reported separately as a cancellation rate (— of scheduled flights).
  3. Times are hhmm integers, not timestamps. 1435 means 14:35, and 2400 is a legal value meaning midnight. Departure hour is derived with integer division and a modulo, and durations come from ActualElapsedTime rather than subtracting arrival from departure, because overnight flights arrive at a smaller clock value than they departed.
  4. Two-letter carrier codes have been reassigned between airlines historically. We key on DOT_ID_Reporting_Airline and treat the letter code as a display label only.

Interaction and animation plan requirement 3

One selection model ties the views together: choosing an airport on the map filters every other view to that airport. The five explanatory dimensions become controls on a single argument rather than five separate stories.

Visualization 1 — map

Click to select an airport and broadcast it to the other views; shift-click to compare two. A month slider re-bins the circles, with radius and fill transitioning over about 400 ms so a viewer can see which airports move seasonally instead of re-reading a new map. A toggle switches the encoded measure between median delay, 15-minute delay rate, and cancellation rate, because an airport that looks calm on one can look severe on another. Route arcs for the selected airport fade in on selection, so the flow layer never competes with the symbol layer by default.

Visualization 2 — heatmap

Filters to the selected airport, with cells interpolating from the national pattern to the local one so the difference is legible as movement. Brushing a range of cells filters Visualization 3 to just those departure hours, which is how we intend to test whether the late-inbound share grows through the day. A month stepper animates January to December on a play control, held to roughly one second per frame.

Visualization 3 — cause composition

Clicking a cause segment re-sorts the carriers by that cause's share, with rows transitioning to their new positions rather than jumping, so the reordering is readable. Toggling between carrier view and airport view keeps the same encoding while changing the unit of analysis. Hovering a segment dims the other four, since at 100% width the comparison a viewer wants is across rows within one cause.

Planned additions (Visualizations 4 and 5)

A distance-versus-delay view with a brushable distance range, and the single-aircraft chain view, where legs animate in sequence along a time axis so accumulation is shown as it happens rather than asserted. The aircraft shown will be selected by a stated rule — the tail numbers with the most legs on a given high-delay day — rather than picked by hand, so the example cannot be a cherry-picked one.

Evaluation plan requirement 4

What we will evaluate

  • Comprehension. Can a first-time viewer read the correct value and the correct trend from each view without our narration?
  • Argument transfer. After using the site, can a viewer state where delay mostly comes from? This is the one that matters: the views can each be readable and the conclusion still fail to land.
  • Interaction discoverability. Do viewers find the airport selection and the cross-filtering without being told it exists?
  • Performance. Time to first render and frame rate during transitions, on a laptop and on a phone.

How

Task-based testing with 6–8 participants drawn from classmates and other students with no aviation background, run individually for about 15 minutes, thinking aloud. Each session uses a fixed task list, for example: find the airport with the highest median delay in July; say whether a 7 a.m. or a 7 p.m. departure is the safer booking, and why; name the carrier most exposed to inherited delay. We record completion, time, and the wording of wrong answers, because how someone is wrong tells us which encoding misled them.

We will also run a short heuristic pass ourselves against the course's design principles — expressiveness and effectiveness of each encoding, colour discriminability, and whether any view invites a comparison the data does not support.

What we will collect

  • Task completion and error rate per task, and time on task
  • A one-sentence written answer to "where does flight delay mostly come from?" collected before and after use, so we can see whether the site moved the belief
  • Think-aloud notes, tagged by which view triggered the confusion
  • Self-rated confidence, 1–5, per task
  • Browser performance timings from the Performance panel

Findings get logged in docs/evaluation.md with the design change each one produced, so the final write-up can show what testing actually changed rather than reporting that testing happened.
