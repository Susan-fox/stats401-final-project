Dataset Data acquisition & analysis

Raw data

Twelve monthly files from the BTS Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance database, covering January–December 2024. Each file is one row per scheduled domestic flight operated by a carrier accounting for at least 0.5% of domestic passenger revenue.

  • Source: BTS TranStats, database EFD ( field definitions )
  • Acquisition: scripted download of the pre-packaged monthly archives from the /PREZIP/ endpoint rather than the interactive form
  • Volume: ≈7 million rows × 110 columns, about 2.5 GB uncompressed
  • Secondary: OpenFlights airport database , trimmed to US airports with IATA codes, for the latitude and longitude that BTS does not supply

The raw files are not committed. data/raw/ is in .gitignore ; scripts/build_data.py regenerates everything downstream of it.

Processing completed

We read only the variables needed for our questions, using DuckDB to query the CSVs directly rather than loading the full dataset into browser memory. Several issues required explicit processing decisions.

  1. Delay-cause columns are conditionally populated. They carry values only when arrival delay reaches 15 minutes. Blank is not zero, so cause shares are calculated over flights with reported delay causes rather than over all flights.
  2. Cancelled and diverted flights have null arrival delay. They are excluded from ordinary arrival-delay statistics and tracked separately as cancellation or diversion outcomes.
  3. Flight times are stored as hhmm integers. For example, 1435 means 14:35 and 2400 represents midnight. Scheduled and actual timing are converted carefully so that overnight flights and sequential aircraft legs can be represented correctly.
  4. Aircraft sequences require reliable flight ordering. Flights are grouped by Tail_Number and date, ordered by scheduled departure time, and checked for airport continuity before being used in the aircraft-chain view.

Visualization Story five connected views

The five visualizations move from aggregate patterns toward increasingly detailed evidence. The story asks: where delays occur, when they accumulate, what causes carriers report, whether the pattern changes with route length , and finally whether delay can be observed moving through successive flights operated by the same aircraft .

Interaction and Exploration meaningful user tasks

The final design uses interaction to support comparison, inspection, filtering, and changing views rather than treating tooltips as the only form of interaction.

Visualization 1 — airport map

Airport symbols provide details on demand through hover. The planned linked version will allow users to select airports and compare delay measures across the other views. Additional controls can switch between median delay, 15-minute delay rate, and cancellation rate.

Visualization 2 — temporal heatmap

Hovering individual cells provides exact values for a specific weekday and departure hour. The temporal matrix supports direct comparison across time, and future linked filtering can restrict the view to a selected airport or period.

Visualization 3 — cause composition

Cause segments can support highlighting and carrier comparison. The final interaction design will allow users to focus on a specific cause and re-rank carriers so differences become easier to inspect.

Visualization 4 — route length

Hovering a distance group provides exact values for flight count, median delay, interquartile range, 90th-percentile delay, delay rate, and late-aircraft share. The distribution design supports comparison across route-length categories rather than reducing each group to a single average.

Visualization 5 — aircraft chain

A dropdown allows users to switch between selected aircraft-day sequences. Hovering an actual-flight segment reveals scheduled and actual timing, departure delay, arrival delay, and flight distance. This interaction moves the project from aggregate evidence to detailed inspection of individual aircraft operations.

Evaluation Plan user testing

What we will evaluate

  • Comprehension. Can a first-time viewer correctly read values and identify the intended pattern from each visualization without narration?
  • Story comprehension. After using the site, can viewers explain how the five views connect from geographic and temporal patterns to reported causes and aircraft-level propagation?
  • Interaction discoverability. Can users discover selection, hover, changing views, and linked exploration without excessive instruction?
  • Performance. Does the visualization load and respond smoothly on ordinary laptop and mobile screens?

How

We plan to conduct task-based testing with 6–8 classmates or other students who do not have specialized aviation knowledge. Sessions will last approximately 15 minutes and use a think-aloud protocol. Example tasks include identifying an airport with unusually high delay, comparing morning and evening departure patterns, identifying a carrier with high late-aircraft share, and interpreting how delay changes across a selected aircraft chain.

We will also conduct a heuristic review using visualization principles from the course, including expressiveness, effectiveness, appropriate visual channels, color discriminability, interaction usefulness, and consistency of the storytelling flow.

What we will collect

  • Task completion and error rate
  • Time on task
  • Think-aloud comments and points of confusion
  • Self-rated confidence from 1–5
  • Short written interpretation of the project's main finding
  • Browser performance observations

Evaluation findings will be documented in docs/evaluation.md together with any design changes that result from the testing.

From Symptoms to Source

Taken together, the five views move from symptoms toward mechanism. The airport map shows where delay is concentrated. The temporal heatmap shows when delay grows. The cause composition identifies how carriers attribute delay. The route-length analysis tests whether the pattern can be explained simply by longer trips. Finally, the aircraft-chain view provides a concrete way to inspect whether delay persists across successive flights operated by the same aircraft. The final version will strengthen this story further through coordinated interactions, annotations, and user evaluation.
