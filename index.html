US Flight Delays: From Symptoms to Source

Flight delays are easy to notice but harder to explain. This project uses more than seven million scheduled U.S. domestic flights from 2024 to move from visible delay patterns toward their possible operational sources.

Questions

The visualization story follows five related questions:

  1. Which airports and regions experience the highest levels of delay?
  2. How do delay patterns vary by time of day and weekday?
  3. How does route distance relate to the severity and variability of delay?
  4. What share of reported delay is associated with late aircraft, carrier operations, the national airspace system, weather, and security?
  5. Can delay propagation be observed across successive flights operated by the same aircraft?

Dataset acquisition and processing

Primary data

The primary dataset is the 2024 Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance database published by the U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics. The data are distributed as monthly files, with one row for each scheduled domestic flight reported by qualifying carriers.

  • Source: BTS TranStats
  • Field definitions: BTS variable documentation
  • Acquisition: the twelve 2024 monthly archives were downloaded from the BTS pre-packaged download endpoint and processed programmatically
  • Volume: — scheduled flights

Airport coordinates

BTS does not provide latitude and longitude in the flight records, so airport coordinates were joined from the OpenFlights airport database using IATA airport codes.

  • Secondary source: OpenFlights airport database

Processing decisions

The raw files are too large to load directly in a browser, so Python and DuckDB are used to select, clean, aggregate, and export only the information needed by the visualizations.

  1. Delay causes are conditionally reported. CarrierDelay, WeatherDelay, NASDelay, SecurityDelay, and LateAircraftDelay are populated for sufficiently delayed flights. Missing cause values are therefore not treated as ordinary zeroes when calculating cause composition.
  2. Cancelled and diverted flights require separate treatment. Flights without a normal arrival-delay value are excluded from ordinary arrival-delay summaries and tracked separately through cancellation and diversion outcomes. The annual cancellation rate is — .
  3. Scheduled times are encoded as hhmm values. Values such as 1435 represent 14:35, while overnight sequences require explicit handling so that later legs remain correctly ordered in time.
  4. Aircraft sequences are constructed from tail numbers. Flights are grouped by Tail_Number and date, ordered by scheduled departure, and checked for continuous airport sequences before being included in the aircraft-chain visualization.

How to Explore

The five views are designed as one connected story rather than as independent charts. Hover over marks for exact values, use the controls above individual charts to change the comparison, and click an airport on the map to connect the geographic and temporal views.

  • Map: switch between delay rate and cancellation rate, or click an airport to filter the heatmap. Click the same airport again to return to the national view.
  • Cause chart: click a cause button to re-sort carriers by that cause. Hovering a cause highlights the same category across carriers.
  • Aircraft chain: use the dropdown to inspect different aircraft-day sequences.

Visualization Story five connected views

Implemented Interactions coordinated exploration

Interaction is used for comparison, filtering, changing views, highlighting, and details on demand. The project does not rely on tooltips alone.

1. Geographic metric switching

Users can switch the state map between 15-minute delay rate and cancellation rate. The state colors and legend transition to the newly selected measure while airport volumes remain fixed.

2. Map-to-heatmap linked filtering

Clicking an airport on the map sends the selected airport to the temporal heatmap. Clicking the airport again removes the selection and returns to the national pattern.

3. Heatmap reset and details

The heatmap displays the active geographic selection and includes a reset control. Hovering individual cells reveals exact median delay, delay rate, flight count, weekday, and scheduled departure hour.

4. Cause-based carrier sorting

Clicking one of the five reported delay causes reorders the carrier rows according to that category's share. Animated transitions preserve visual continuity during the change in ranking.

5. Cause highlighting

Hovering one delay category emphasizes that category across the carrier chart and fades competing categories, supporting direct comparison across rows.

6. Aircraft-day selection

A dropdown changes the aircraft-day sequence shown in the final visualization. This lets users compare multiple examples rather than relying on a single manually chosen aircraft.

7. Details on demand

Hover tooltips throughout the project provide exact values without permanently adding labels that would clutter the visualizations.

Design Rationale

Each visualization uses an idiom matched to a different analytical task. Geographic position is used for airport and regional comparison; a matrix supports repeated temporal comparison; normalized stacked bars support part-to-whole comparison; distribution summaries show both typical and extreme delay; and a sequence timeline represents the operational order of flights.

Color is deliberately restrained. The map and heatmap use sequential ramps because their measures have quantitative order. Delay causes use a small categorical palette, with related colors distinguishing categories associated with the airline's own operation from external conditions. The aircraft timeline uses color primarily to distinguish more severe arrival delay from flights that are near or ahead of schedule.

Because flight-delay distributions are highly skewed, several views emphasize median, quartiles, and upper percentiles. This reduces the risk that a small number of extreme events dominate the interpretation of the typical flight.

Evaluation

The project can be evaluated through task-based testing with users who have general familiarity with charts but no specialized aviation knowledge. Evaluation should focus on whether viewers can understand the visual encodings, discover the interactions, and correctly describe the relationship among the five views.

Representative tasks include:

  • identify a state or airport with relatively high delay
  • compare morning and evening delay patterns
  • identify which carriers have relatively high shares of a selected delay cause
  • describe how delay variability changes across distance groups
  • determine whether delay increases, persists, or recovers across an aircraft sequence

Useful evaluation measures include task accuracy, completion time, interaction errors, self-reported confidence, and qualitative comments about confusing encodings or controls.

From Symptoms to Source

The five views form a progressively narrower investigation of U.S. flight delays. The map begins with the geographic distribution of delay. The heatmap reveals how performance changes across the operating day and week. The cause chart shifts attention from visible outcomes to the categories carriers use to explain delay. The distance view tests whether delay behavior can be explained simply by route length. Finally, the aircraft-chain view moves from aggregate statistics to the sequence of flights operated by one physical aircraft.

Together, these views show why a delayed flight should not always be interpreted as an isolated event. In a tightly connected airline network, the outcome observed on one flight may reflect conditions and disruptions carried forward from earlier operations. The interactive structure lets users move between system-level patterns and specific operational examples rather than relying on a single summary statistic.

Reproducibility

The project source code, processing scripts, visualization code, processed data, and documentation are available in the project repository.

The large raw BTS monthly files are not stored directly in GitHub. After downloading the raw files into data/raw/ , the processed visualization files can be regenerated with:

python scripts/build_data.py

The website uses HTML, CSS, JavaScript, D3.js v7, TopoJSON, Python, and DuckDB.
